Twenty-four is a rather inexplicable age, she said.

At the very moment she blew out the candles, it occurred to her what a wonderful word *inexplicable* was. It demanded so little. It relieved one of the responsibility for things too heavy to be carried lightly. To call it suffering seemed rather self-indulgent; to call it happiness would be something of a lie. But inexplicable. Yes, inexplicable would do.

It reminded her of a photograph by Fan Ho. The woman in the qipao might have been twenty-four herself. There was a high wall, a fierce sun, and a great shadow falling obliquely across half the wall, as though pressing down upon it. At its foot stood the woman, precisely where the light ended and the shadow began. Whether she was hiding from the sun or from the shadow, nobody could tell.

As a child, she had always imagined twenty-four to be the age of the grown-ups in books and soap operas. Grown-ups were people who knew where they would live, what work they would do, what was worth keeping, and how to come to terms with all the parts of life they had themselves chosen to delete.

And yet, when the clock struck midnight and she became twenty-four, there was nothing. No sudden understanding of the world, no particular revelation. Only a vast, unaccountable bewilderment. She sat before a screen, doing nothing at all.

The woman in the photograph was still there.

Perhaps growing up was rather like filling out a death certificate, line by line, for a body whose cause of death nobody knew. Or perhaps it was watching that body slowly decay before one's own eyes, knowing all the while that nothing could be done.

She told her fiancé, I want to order a cake. And a piece of paper that can be set alight, with the lyrics I choose printed upon it.

Twenty-four. You and I have both become twenty-four.

Perhaps human beings invented numbers simply to compress what could not be borne into something small enough to look at. For twenty-four, after all, in the vast and measureless universe, was nothing more than two Arabic numerals.

A little fire, a single breath, and all of it could be blown out.

Happy twenty-fourth birthday to her.
