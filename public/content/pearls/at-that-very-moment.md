It was cold in Amir's house. The cold was everywhere. Naomi huddled in a chair, eating the food he had made for her. In Persian, he told her that to cook for someone was to spare their hands from harm. She curled her tongue, trying to echo the foreignness in his voice, but the trilled *R* sounded ridiculous every time.

Naomi had come from Korea. She was of Chinese and Japanese descent. She was always telling him, you white assholes know nothing of our East Asian sorrow. Whenever she called him white, he frowned. I'm half, he'd say. Iranian and German. Once she told him she was trying to look at certain things without her East Asian blood getting in the way. He smiled as though a breeze had just passed. A good idea, he said, but you'll find there's no escaping it. In Beijing they thought he was American. In America they thought he was from Dubai. In England he was English.

Naomi lay on the floor reading the *I Ching*, using Zhuge's Maqian divination to work out whether he would die of lung cancer. Once again she felt she understood what the universe was. He sat smoking ha$h while she lay on the floor with her vape. Suddenly she told him she said I love you to everyone, just to see how they would react.

He laughed and called her toxic. One day, he said, it would fail her. Perhaps one day she would say I love you and really mean it, but having said it so often, she might no longer recognise it as the truth herself. Like the boy who cried wolf.

Of course, Naomi told him luv u, too.

Il se couvrit le visage et eut un petit rire.

Bro, you know that's a lie, she said.

He looked at her and said, but perhaps it was true at that very moment.

Naomi treated I love you like *jiǔ niàng*, sweet fermented rice wine. When she said it, she felt none of the embarrassment of saying it in her mother tongue. Drunk late at night, she would tell everyone she passed in the street, i love u.

Love, how vast you are.

When Naomi was floating among the clouds, when she lay in his room one afternoon whispering that she was fishing on a boat, there was the hand that cupped her right cheek. She mistook that for love.

At that moment she really did love him, Naomi thought.

Loved the bones of his brow, loved his eyelashes, so beautiful they looked almost fake, loved the way he talked and talked without stopping, loved that half the songs on his playlist were songs she liked. Loved his curly hair, his idealism, the Lenin tattoo on his arm. Loved that life was an ouroboros. Loved the way he brushed his teeth with his mouth crooked to one side, no different from a little boy. Loved how his faintly cool lips, damp with moisture, carried her into London at the end of May, still freezing.

Naomi kissed his almost nonexistent upper lip, just as she kissed the breakdown she had lived with for so long. That morning she took Plan B. She knew the mine disaster had already happened somewhere inside her.

Western feng shui amounted to little more than placing a screen between a bookcase and a bed. And across the bedsheets, amid all the crossings and manoeuvres, fell a copy of the *I Ching*, long since drained of its flavour.

After that May, they no longer saw each other.
