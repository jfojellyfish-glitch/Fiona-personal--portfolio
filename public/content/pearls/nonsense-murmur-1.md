I had thought that once the dissertation was finished, I might at last be still. Yet when the writing is done, there is no stopping; when the project is done, there is no rest either. There is always another thing, and another, as though the days were fastening themselves together without leaving so much as a breath between them.

Sometimes I wish to be nothing more than a jellyfish, quiet in the depths of the sea, with no need to think at all. To shine simply because I am myself. To remain suspended, endlessly, somewhere between sleep and death—an eternal bardo, neither arriving nor departing.

But the moment I fall asleep, the nightmares come. They are like a marsh: soft on the surface, and without a bottom. I have dreamed every night for more than five years. Every night. And now the borders of dreaming and waking have grown so faint that I can scarcely tell one from the other.

And perhaps, after all, there is nothing to tell. For whether it is a dream or what we call reality, both are empty.

Empty, empty.
