萬水千山

In the last month of 2023, I became suddenly aware that my mind was growing dull.

Since the summer had begun to draw to an end, I had started seeing a Polish boy, made two new friends, and now, quite without my noticing, another year was coming to its close. The little tablets I had taken for years, white and pale yellow, building one upon another the semblance of what might be called an ordinary life, I had gradually stopped taking. Yet it occurred to me, rather unexpectedly, that even without opening those packets, without swallowing anything at all, I was becoming numb of my own accord.

This winter was much like the last. I lay in bed and did nothing. Not even my telephone did I touch.

Strangely enough, I spoke to my ex this month. Stranger still, we spoke with such composure.

I told him that my present boyfriend was very good to me, that he treated me well, and yet I could not seem to feel anything.

"What you mean," he said, "is that you're bored with him, aren't you?"

Then I asked how he had been.

"You never understood," he said, "that I'm someone who needs company."

I told him I wanted the pho in Taojin, the little open-air restaurants along the Pearl River, the old teahouses tucked away in the older quarters of the city.

Another year, he said. How quickly it had gone. And still he had not met a girl whom he could take to all those restaurants, the ones he thought so good.

"Chinese people!" I said. "Food is our one eternal love language!"

Though these days, I told him, I hardly cared what I ate. If there was nothing I particularly wanted, I would simply find something, anything, whatever was sufficient to keep the body functioning.

"You weren't like this before," he said. "You used to go on about something you wanted to eat for an entire day if you couldn't have it. Crying, complaining, making such a fuss."

And I thought of that girl, who had once wanted things so terribly.

Earlier that year, I told him, I had been to an island where there was a Vietnamese restaurant, one of the very few, which seemed, come to think of it, rather luxurious compared with the countryside where I lived. It was a little place run by a married couple. The husband was English, the wife Vietnamese. She had once been a nurse in Taiwan and spoke a little Mandarin.

And there they were, after all those thousands of miles, running a pho restaurant in Africa.

The husband was rather funny. He said I sounded American.

Ten thousand rivers, ten thousand mountains.

Across them all, and across them again.

Perhaps that is what love is, I said. To pass through the great crowds of the world, or through places where there is not another soul; to cross rivers and mountains, to wander through all that vastness, until, beyond the dark willows and the bright flowers, there appears another village.

That village.

To have travelled ten thousand rivers and ten thousand mountains, only to arrive, at last, in my promised land.

In this little town in the northeast of Scotland, many people have asked me, or perhaps have asked themselves, whether they will ever come back.

Aberdeen, for most young people, is a forgotten place, a place in which one's youth seems somehow confined. There are times when I do not like this town. One might say, rather generously, that it stands apart from the world, a small and quiet corner of human existence. Or one might simply say that it has none of the bustle of London, nor even Edinburgh.

Everything had happened in such a hurry, Joey said.

But who knows where in this life we might meet again? I said.

And I remembered going to the amusement park with him and Yichi. They told me to keep the tickets, to put them somewhere safe. Perhaps we would never come back, they said, though if we came often enough we could collect points, and the points could be exchanged for prizes.

But I felt about in my pockets, and the points card had gone with the sea wind.

We did not know then how close the parting already was.

In May, when the spring rain was falling, we exchanged books in the cemetery I so often visited. And there we had been, laughing beneath the strange light of the night sky, pointing towards a crossing where hardly anyone passed, saying how dreamlike it looked, how very *dreamcore*, as though we had somehow wandered into a place that existed only in sleep.

And another year had passed.

A whole year, so strangely, like a dream.
